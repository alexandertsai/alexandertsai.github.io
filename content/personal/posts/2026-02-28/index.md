I reached LA at around 9ish today. Abby came by and picked me up. Arik suggested spontaneously that we go to the beach that night and so we did. 

The beach was around a 30 minute drive from her place. When we reached it was around 11 pm. I think one of my toes became frostbitten from how cold it was. We stood near the shoreline with the water crawling to around our waist level, shuffling around trying to stay warm. Arik said that the only way to not feel cold was to dive into these massive waves. And he was kind of right - after diving in I did feel slightly better. 

One thing I noted was the splendor of the sky. I suppose most days after work I really just head back home and don't bother to look upwards too much. But today I had the chance to observe the night sky in all its beauty. The overwhelming darkness of the beach made it so I could clearly see the moon and the specks of stars almost dancing around it. To our right, there lay something akin to a town of houses, some still with their lights on, emitting a faint yellowish glow. It's quite like that scene in Kiki's Delivery Service where she overlooks the town with the ocean view. 

Something I realised today is just how nice it is to be around my sister. Conversation just seems to flow. And that sounds like a strange thing to say because I should know that without having to come down to LA. Your sister is your sister. But I hadn't really gotten the chance to spend much time with Abby, especially in recent years. After she moved to the US we see each other maybe once a year for a few days at most.

I remember a few years back when my parents had left for a trip, Ame and I ended up staying at our cousin's place. After spending some nights there we naturally spoke a bit more to our cousin (who we hadn't spoken to much prior), and ended up having some rather soulful conversations. In a lot of ways that kind of connection wasn't something I expected to form, but its emergence was the best possible thing that could have come out of that scenario. There are definitely times where it can feel pretty isolating, especially if we're constantly shifting around and having to sorta reset our circles each time. But I think we often forget how really great company might just be lurking right around the corner.
