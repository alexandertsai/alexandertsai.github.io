I recently apportioned a great deal of my life savings (100%) into gold. Call me silly. Alex! What about diversification? What about only investing what you're ready to lose? Well I've clearly ignored the fundamentals and now pay the price by spending almost every waking moment watching the spot price of gold change in real time on CNBC. It has been emotional turmoil since yesterday. At first it was up majorly when Trump declared he would use force on Greenland to capture it. Then he later came back to announce that he wouldn't use force after all and had instead came up with a NATO-approved framework. Great for Greenlanders, devastating to me...

*Quick context, political volatility in the US causes people to lose faith and sell US dollars to buy precious metals (gold, silver, platinum) that are seen as more "safe". Put simply, Trump peaceful = gold fall. Trump aggressive = gold surge. 

As I sat at my desk wondering why I felt so stressed, pouring over gold prices and rereading snippets of Trump's duress, I realised that I was really invested in gold. I mean besides the fact I hold gold iShares, I am emotionally and financially invested in the price of gold.

I know how it feels to pour all of yourself into something (a sport, game, skill, event) or someone (lover, love interest, disinterested lover). You blame yourself for not being good enough at Y, you get anxious when X doesn't reply, and it really tears you apart. But then when you succeed (at Y or getting X's reply), ohhoho when you succeed, the most euphoric feeling surges over you - tonnes of weights fly off your shoulders and you feel great. You are the best at Y. Person X is so into you. You're smart, you're funny, you're everything everyone wants to be.

Then maybe due to some unfortunate set of events, the next reply from X doesn't come within the number of minutes you expect it to. Or maybe you get rejected from a posting requiring specialisation in Y (but I thought I was great at Y). OR MAYBE the price of gold drops by A WHOLE PERCENT wiping out a considerable amount of your life savings. You suddenly don't feel so smart. I really wasted my time talking to Y. I still need to brush up on X. I shouldn't have bought so much damn gold. 

Any event whom's worth is derived from its intrinsic uncertainty is usually difficult to model. Will person Y always reply quickly and with zest? It's hard to say - unless you're really really sure about how person Y feels about you (but if so that euphoric surge would probably would be closer to a speck of joy). Are you actually good at X? Well although that's, to an extent, within your control, if the satisfaction derived from X depends on something like landing a certain job, in which a great number of chance factors (such as the interviewer's son-in-law eyeing the same posting) influence that result, are you really in control? And also will gold go up? Ngl idk

My message here is that investing yourself heavily into something largely outside your sphere of influence is foolish. Especially that which is **uncertain**. If stock prices on some fundamental level were really just brownian motion, you'd live happily only every other day. If all your worth in life is derived from someone else, when some tragic instance bestows itself upon said person (car accident maybe), you'll be crowned the first remotely conjoined twin, because you'll wither in pain as you wonder why they're silent for hours.

This advice by itself is trite. Everyone knows not to put too many eggs into a basket (although I am surprised by how often I lapse into that very habit). But my desired message isn't really about the investing principal but rather the surprising amount of contexts we can find ourselves doing this and why it can be so so addictive. It's having so much skin in the game that any slight change can trigger an emotional hurricane. You'll feel over the moon or like you've been hit by the moon...

There are very few extremes that aren't volatile. Hedge funds posting massive returns are also perfectly capable of going under just as fast. Your favourite people in life are also those most capable of inflicting misery. I sometimes find myself making extraordinarily greedy plays, hoping to win the greatest amount in the greatest way. Thinking back, the times when they had payed off don't immediately come to mind, but them backfiring is still clear as day. I suppose the realisation here is that to aspire to feel the highest highs, one should ensure such a thing is mostly certain and within their influence, else they should ready themselves to feel the lowest lows too.
