### Getting Organised

I have approximately a year and a half before I graduate. To make good use of that time, it's important to be organised. There are often hours that slip because you just don't really know **exactly** what you should be doing at that moment. This is usually followed by a crucial moment down the line where you wish you had done more of something before such a tight timeline came along. The following is what I believe to be crucial!

1. Make and maintain good friendships
2. Score as well as possible on my remaining courses
3. Do well in internships by working hard and continuing to learn outside of work timings
4. Prepare for GREs
5. Stay fit

I like to write things down to keep myself accountable. I don't mean for this blog to become my personal to-do list but I didn't really have any other place to put this... Although I will say I think a lot of goals invariably compound in one way or another. For example, exercise has been shown to improve cognitive function and mental health. These will undoubtably be a chief factor in 2, 3, and 4 -  activities that require high amounts of cognitive ability and mental health. For the most part, my schedule over the next 8 months is fixed. I know I will go into work at this time and I will leave at some later time. My goal here is to establish a simple routine that allows me to move towards these goals gradually. 

For 1, I think I'm already trying my best so that's fine :) For 2, the most important thing I believe will be preparation in advance. There are a few courses (namely the ones to do with stats) that I'm worried I won't be able to secure an A in, primarily because I am a very careless person. I may have the ability to do the questions but I am really, really susceptible to a smorgasbord of silly mistakes, even including things such as reading the question wrong (I read blood type AB as A in one of my final exams)... So, to prevent this, I believe the only way is to really dilligently practice the material in advance and sharpen my mind in some sense. This is something I know will take a time. The first potentially worrying course is CS103 (Linear Algebra). I'm generally not too concerned about programming courses because at least you can run the code and easily verify the output. But courses that require you to write things on a sheet of paper frighten me. 

For 3, while this is something I will do anyway, upon reflecting on my previous internship, I realise that what I lacked was the continuous desire to learn. This was especially in relation to a lot of the documents that would get shared around. The compounding effect of these documents being in chinese and their technicalities being slightly out of my reach made them really, really hard to read. After a bit I lost motivation. This lack of reading was also encouraged by things like the coding environment getting set up, resulting in your primary language of concern to switch from chinese to python (lovely). I also mananged to scrape by by asking some of my Singaporean colleagues about whatever crucial concepts I couldn't grasp.  But now there are no such angels! Hence, I think the actionable from here is to adopt the continuous-chinese-learning mindset. This is what I did near the start in Singapore, but mostly stopped in the second week when work picked up.

For 4, I have more confidence in the quantitative section rather than the English one. After some googling, I've realised one of the best things to prepare now is to read a lot and expand my vocabulary. I usually do not like resorting to vocab banks but I might eventually have to open one. For the time being, I have already begun reading on the Kindle and am highlighting the words I do not know! I am currently reading The Remains Of The Day by Kazuo Ishiguro, one of my (if not my) all time favourite authors. I really, really enjoyed Never Let Me Go and Klara and The Sun (the latter which I finished in two sittings!) and I've been enjoying this one too so far. It's honestly quite incredible how he manages to portray the thoughts of a butler in such a believable and lucid way. I am not sure if he has ever been a butler for Darlington-or-a-similarly-prestigious Hall but regardless, I find it to be extremely well written. Sometimes he also goes out of his way to describe rather simple phenomena.  I'm not sure if this was the intent but I find those sections really funny. Anyway, I will probably do a short post on my thoughts about the book after I finish it. It will be the first book I finish after leaving behing a trail of unfinished books (if I finish it, which I hope I do) in a very long time.

Lastly for 5, my plan is that everyday, at around 3 or 4 pm, I shall head down to the gym and do some exercises. It's free anyway so I should make use of that. I've also signed up for a spin class on Sunday morning! They've labelled this class under "Empower", which refers to a class that is supposedly (according to the description they added) very uncomplicated and mostly sticks to the saddle. I haven't spun in quite a while so I thought this would be a good re-introduction to the world of spin. That and all the other classes had been booked. If this one is good I think I'll also try for one on a weekday because it's only a 10 minutes walk from where I stay.

I hope your goals are going great reader. I'll update soon!
