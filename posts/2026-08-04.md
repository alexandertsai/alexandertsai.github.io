### Different Strokes for Different Folks 

The other day I had a conversation with my friend Wooz. We went over our usual spiel - third cultures, our experiences at work, school etc. We then got to the point where we started talking about our friends. He was telling me about how his friend had recently shared about his honeymoon experience. That friend's fiancee had already planned every detail down to the wire. We are going here. We are going to do this. Even for their engagement, she had stated that he should propose to her at this location with this ring. Wooz's response to hearing this was complete disbelief. It was clear that it was impossible for him to process.

Now, I can imagine most of my readers would react similarly. Can you really call that a honeymoon? Where is the element of thinking about what the other person wants and showing your love by putting those thoughts into action? But also, maybe another portion of my readers will understand her actions completely. These two fractions of my readers are *different*. Today, I want to talk a bit about different people and my general thoughts. 

When I met my fiancee Yujin, our conversations were fluid but didn't quite go beyond surface level. It was only after we grew closer and encountered difficulties did we start to have much deeper interactions that, in my opinion, are a much clearer indicator of the type of person we are and how we think about things. She is in, a lot of ways, very different from me.

Our MBTIs may not be so far off, but there are a lot of instances where I have difficulty wrapping my head around what she might say or do. This would at times cause conflicts that more stemmed from misunderstandings than fundamental wrongdoings. Ideas expressed in different ways and intentions swept aside. I might think the ends don't justify the means (intention-orientated) and she might think otherwise (outcome-orientated).

But truthfully, after spending more time together, I've come to realise that the beauty in our conversations wasn't necessarily that we got each other completely, but that we tried really hard to and, through that process, actually learned things and strengthened our relationship.

I was once asked the hypothetical question about whether I'd choose a partner that understood me well - they'd know what I want and would agree with me more often than not - or a partner that didn't quite understand me completely, but gave their very best to do so.

Ignoring the fact that the former is incredibly difficult to chance upon, I would always choose the latter. Relationships are not just about the so-called "natural affinity" that strikes you when you meet the person, but rather the level of effort and commitment two people are willing to put into one another. There is a well-studied phenomenon that many people who excel in certain subjects when young tend to give up more easily later in life when they encounter hurdles. This is because they're not used to resistance, and on a deeper level, perhaps their love for the subject may not have come from the subject itself, but rather their proficiency over others at it.

This also reminds me of how a friend of mine used to say they disliked people who cited "incompatibility" as a reason to break up, stating that while there are valid cases, it was, more often than not, an excuse for not wanting to put more effort into the relationship. To a good extent, I agree. 

Does that mean you must intentionally meet people who are diametrically opposed to your line of thought? Radicals? The deranged? The unsound? Not necessarily. But there is some merit to occasionally talking to people who you don't completely gel with initially, because it's more likely you'll learn something new from them than with someone that is simply your a pseudo-yes-man. 

So with all that said, I wanted to talk about people who are different - specifically loving people (whether as friends or as partners) who are different. Over time, I've realised that the conviction I have in my relationship didn't stem from the electricity I felt at the start, but rather the lengths we go to to make each other feel heard and understood. In any relationship, you will be tested at some point, and the relationship and yourself will be under strain. It is in those moments, now and later, that I thank myself for choosing to remain open to people who aren't quite myself. It has helped me find someone that will stay with me through the good, the bad, and everything else in-between. I'd say this is simply because what we love isn't how frictionless our relationship is, that initial spark, or our degree of "compatibility", but rather each other.
