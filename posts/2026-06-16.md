Recently I've been thinking about what it means to be alive. The literal meaning is obvious - your reading of this post is just that. But people often use the phrase to describe something much more. They often express wistfulness over the faint lack of "feeling alive" in recent times. "Ah I can't remember the last time I really felt alive" or "It's been dull recently". They didn't die by any means but perhaps they are saying a part of them did.

To try and pin down what it means to feel alive is a rather arduous task. The issue is that there isn't really a clear barrier on being alive or not. There are some days that are more lively than others but that doesn't mean they've crossed the "alive" threshold. My conclusion was to simply assume that everyone has some vague notion of what it means to be alive, within all that vagueness take the rough median, and write this post based on that. 

I distinctly remember talking to my counsellor in junior college, telling her that the past year or so hasn't felt very alive. When asked to expound, I stated that it seemed as if all the colours in my life had turned dull. I hadn't become profoundly colourblind but something about the colours didn't quite pop like they used to. I guess that quote was good because my counsellor asked me later if I minded her quoting me in the future.

So what does it mean to be alive? I have a collection of hazy memories - a mix of going up different mountains and breathing the mountainous air and embracing the view that comes with the summit. I have memories of running with all my might down unfamiliar paths, not knowing where I'll end up but that it'll probably be somewhere interesting. I remember particularly good times with loved ones, where we weren't doing something so casual as catching up over a dinner, but rather cycling somewhere as the sun set, exploring a completely foreign environment in languages we barely understood, or just sitting around one another talking about ideas we were so passionate about.

But if we were to try to be more precise I'd say feeling alive is wanting to be in the exact place that you are as you are. Forget the phone, the obligations, the need to pee, but just being present. Remember the scenery surrounding you (be it people or trees or walls), the intense beating in your heart, that faint delusion that you might just fly if you were to leap now. You can't really think about feeling alive when you feel alive. You're so absorbed into that moment that the world almost shrinks around you.

Obviously feeling alive is a wonderful feeling and we'd like to feel that all the time. While there isn't really any clear way to replicate this I'd say one thing I've noticed is that it typically involves novelty. We're doing something we haven't done or talking to people we don't know yet we really want to. Another aspect, and this might sound strange, but usually the period of your life surrounding that window of being alive isn't very happy. I think this makes sense to an extent - if you were already feeling happy and content then that sudden jolt in energy and joy might not awaken you quite enough. Lastly, the choice being made can't be completely rational. Something about that choice has to be sub-optimal in a sense. It can't be a horrible, horrible choice but it needs to be something that isn't guaranteed to work out. 

Three months ago I developed a giant crush. Prior to that, I had been just living my life in San Jose. The life wasn't bad by any means but it was rather dull. I went to work from 10:30am to around 11pm daily. I'd head home, shower, reply to texts and sleep. But truthfully it was incredibly lonely. I've had the privilege of going on now three internships in the US despite studying in Singapore, and each of them has begun in rather solitary fashions. I'm a pretty social person so I usually am able to pull myself out of that, but it always starts out a bit sad. After meeting that person and getting to know her better and better, I started to guess that I might just like her. I mean it's very easy to conflate liking someone and enjoying their company. But as time went on I became more and more sure.

As my feelings actualised, the cogs in my mind began to whir. I also knew that my internship would end in two months. The obviously logical thing to do would be to detach and soften the impact. But I said no. I said I would get as emotionally invested as I want and in the likely event that nothing worked out, I'd just be devastated and that was that. And that's when I really felt alive. That's when being around this person made all the colours flourish. That's when my pupils dilated and I could take in the sheer vividness of what was unfolding in front of me.

I think it's rather difficult to artificially manufacture situations where you know you'll feel alive. There are ways to make those situations more likely (e.g. by being brave and talking to strangers or going to new places) but you can't force it in some sense. When it comes it comes. But I think that's what makes those brief moments or periods so, so precious. Look alive!
