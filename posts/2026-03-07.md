### The Girl Who Leapt Through Time

I originally wanted to write this post with NO spoilers but as I was writing I came to realise I will indeed leak a tiny spoiler (the first twenty minutes or so) so I'm really sorry. You can close this now if you want. 3, 2, 1... okay time waits for no one, continuing with the post! To give some context, this movie is essentially about a girl who gains the ability to leap through time and then realises welp actually there are a ton of complications when it comes to that.

The movie starts with Makoto (the girl) waking up late and rushing to school. She has what most people would call a bad day. Her misfortune includes: being stumped by a quiz in the morning (leaving it empty), her cooking catching fire, and a boy being thrown at her. Throughout the day, she constantly states that she is a lucky person, and even after each terrible incident occurs, she doubles down and goes "but tis was an exception". As she heads home, Makoto is descending a hill on her bike when her brakes begin to malfunction. As she plummets down the hill, she panics and realises she had forgotten to account for how today was a really unlucky day. As she speeds towards the bottom, a train crossing appears. The barriers slowly shut due to an oncoming train. Unable to stop, she hits the barrier, flies into the air, and the next scene plays out in slow motion as we see she is about to collide with that train.

During this flying scene, Makoto thinks to herself: "Oh, I am going to die. If I knew I was going to die today I would have woke up earlier, I would have avoided that boy being thrown at me..." and so on and so forth. 

Okay cut! So that's the start of the movie. While I deem the animation and build up to this part masterful, what I really thought about after watching the movie was her dialogue in that instant. I believe what makes this moment so special is that it's clear she didn't have a clue prior to this that she was going to die. I mean most people who die usually do so with some premonition beforehand. Like, oh I've got this disease, or I've become really old, or something like that. Curiously, in her last moments, she isn't thinking about major events that occurred several years ago like "I wish I told this boy how I felt about him", but rather about events on that very day. How she wish she woke up earlier, dodged the boy being thrown at her, studied for that quiz etc.

My initial reaction upon viewing this scene was to contemplate the possibility of living every day as if you didn't know you were going to die later that day. I know it sounds a bit morbid, but if I had thought that today I was going to die, I think I'd have woken up a bit earlier and done my laundry first.

Upon more contemplation, the phrase "Live every day as if it's your last" emerged, usually referring to the importance of being spontaneous and immersed in the moment. But I don't believe that is quite what the movie intends to convey. To me, the subtle difference lies in what are realistic attitudes and what aren't. I mean if I really lived every day as if it was my last I would have run out of money by Tuesday. Rather, I think this movie alludes more to "live every day as you can best, because you're not sure when it'll be your last". 

I translate this as taking up small (not large) opportunities that have always been present. Like if you really enjoy being around someone you should ask them to hang out. You don't have to confess your love or retrieve a wedding ring, but just do the small and harmless things. Wake up a bit earlier so you don't have to uber to the office and hide from your boss in the lobby. Go finish up the laundry you've been putting off. Say good morning to strangers and elicit a mixed reaction of confusion and annoyance. I think that's all okay to do. 

At the start of this movie there is a quote written on the board (that I've also quoted in my first paragraph) that goes "Time waits for no one". For a surpising amount of people, I'd say what holds true is that it isn't really about how much time you have, but how you spend it. There are lots of interpretations on how you might make each day it's best and I can't really say for sure how my dear reader should do that, but I hope reading this post serves as a good catalyst to start trying :)
