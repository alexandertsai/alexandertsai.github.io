### Maybe you should give up

I recently saw this [clip](https://www.reddit.com/r/TikTokCringe/comments/1v2ws5n/streamer_gives_beginners_a_realistic_presentation/) featuring a streamer called Ludwig. He was teaching at "streamer university", a program where a select group of up-and-coming streamers attend classes by successful streamers. Although for the most part this is not really a real university and classes can be serious, Ludwig had been teaching this one quite seriously. This clip shows him discussing some statistics regarding the revenue breakdown between streamers of different levels. He shows how despite being in the top 1% of streamers, many people still struggle to earn a living. His ultimate message to the class was that they weren't cut out to be a streamer, because it's an overwhelming amount of work and luck to grow one's channel to the levels they dream about.

Obviously this is a cruel message. But I also think this is a reality seen in many different aspects of our lives. In a world of startups receiving ludicrous amounts of funding, people often fail to notice the overwhelming number of them that fail. The rewards are exponentially distributed. A small percentage of people reap most of the rewards, while the vast majority don't get much.

Many people often possess lofty dreams of being the best at something. It's okay to be delusional (in fact I'd say being delusional is a requirement for some fields), but I don't think one should be delusional and lazy. People often underestimate the amount of work required to accomplish a certain feat, yet feel dissatisfied when they don't achieve that thing. I've observed these same people vacillating between their "dream" and their other interests, and ultimately electing to scroll on their phones. 

This post isn't to say most people can't be successful without slaving away their entire lives. There are many professions that pay well and are sufficiently interesting that can be pursued without requiring you to dedicate an inordinate amount of time. Although some will require a more significant percentage (e.g. consulting), most definitely can still result in a holistic and fulfilling life. My post more refers to the glamorised professions such as being the very best in one's field, such as playing in the NBA or being a professional gamer or streamer.

However, I don't really believe in the title of this post. That was more clickbait to get you to read (sorry... I'm testing new ways to increase reader retention). I don't think most people should entirely give up on whatever their passion might be. However, it's important to recognise the amount of work needed to succeed in such a thing. If you really, really want something, you have to do the work required to get it. 

I believe the more nuanced part about this whole conundrum is accepting that even hard work may not be enough. There will be someone who was born the son of a director at Goldman Sachs. He's probably going to take your summer internship spot - even if you are more qualified on paper. Yes, there's no helping that. But I do believe most people are usually lucky enough to chance across some opportunity at some point in their life. It might not be now, it might not be next year, but at some point there's going to be a slim crack that opens and nets you the chance to pursue your dreams. It is therefore paramount we work really, really hard for what we love and care about so that when that chance comes (it will come I swear) we are ready.
