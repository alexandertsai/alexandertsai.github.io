### It really is what you make of it

Yesterday I went to a rave at Pier 80 (a warehouse in San Francisco). The idea to go to such an event was a very spontaneous one - I didn't really think about it until I started seeing an influx of EDM-related content appearing on my feed. Eventually I caved and ended up buying Illenium tickets since I figured I could just take the Caltrain there and back. 

I left work at around 6:30 and started travelling down. The entire journey took around 2 hours. When I reached, Crankdat had begun his set (Illenium was scheduled for around 9:30). I wasn't a huge fan of the very heavy bass style but I still enjoyed parts of his set here and there. My first hour there was really more spent walking around following these trains (large lines of people, primarily Asian for some reason, squeezing through the crowd to get somewhere). I kept following different trains in hopes of getting to the front. Which I did! Sorta. I was actually pretty close to Illenium, maybe around twenty-ish meters. But the front firstly was insanely crowded. Like it was actually breathtakingly (LITERALLY) crowded. People are very pushy and there was this guy with his (what I presume to be) girlfriend that kept elbowing the second I was even perceived to be moving towards his girl - even though it is literally not possible to occasionally shift towards a direction because people are always trying to squeeze past here and there.

I eventually leave the front because it was too hot and uncomfortable. I follow another train to head backwards. I stop at a more open space and just spend some time there next to this burly French (?) dude. As I'm moving along to the music, I spot this group of people going absolutely insane. They are like jumping up and down spinning around waving to the sound insane. I end up shifting closer to them and after A LOT of deliberation I ask if I can join (to which they said yes!). That turns out to be the best part of the night. We all go crazy just enjoying Illenium's music, which I honestlyyy don't really think is all that good (sorry...) but with great company, anything rhythmic will probably sound good enough to move to.

The rest is wraps. Just lots of movement and becoming very thirsty and taking the Caltrain back to the office to get some water. The people I met were kind enough to give me a ride, which helped because I just caught the Caltrain and the next would be in an hour.

I think when I reflect back on last night it makes me realise how much of an experience is what you make of it. Not reallyyy about positive mindsets - but more on the actions you decide to take. I don't think this will apply to every experience, but there are a surprising number of events that involve a surprising number of choices. And my realisation here is that there are so many awesome, amazing and lovely things that would never happen if I had never made a particular choice. Some of my closest friends today came about only because I decided to speak to someone I barely knew (a friend of a not-so-close friend). This person happened to be interning at the same company, and upon meeting, had then made more introductions to other interns. My experience last night was also mostly dictated by me - if I hadn't gone up to that group and joined them I probably wouldn't really have enjoyed the night as much as I did.

My point is that I believe it's important to try to make the most of a situation and to try things. There have been a lot of occasions where I wouldn't say I'm downright miserable but I'm not really having a blast either - something which I largely attribute to the absence of good company. Of course making the most of things extends beyond just talking to people you don't know, but I wanted to reflect on this anecdote today because I feel like there are still a couple of areas in life I could make a lot more of, and that I will try my best to in the coming months!
