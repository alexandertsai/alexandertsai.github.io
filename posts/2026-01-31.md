Over the weekend, I attended some Stanford Continuing Studies (essentially classes for adult learners) with my primary motivation being to experience the Stanford experience once. The first hurdle was really reaching the lecture hall. We didn't have an access key to the building so I did a bit of tailgating. Once I found the lecture hall, I headed inside and ended up sitting next to this old guy. 

I later found out that his name is George and he is retired. I wasn't surprised since this class was targeted at adult learners, but he was definitely slightly older than the others in the class (most seemed 30s-40s). We exchanged a few pleasantries and then the class commenced.

The content from the class wasn't really new to me. It was taught by two people from Balyasny, a research engineer and the chief AI officer. I didn't exactly get as much networking out of it as I would have liked either which was a bit sad. But it was a good experience.

Anyway back to George! We were sitting in class running through a Colab notebook on some basic RAG when he runs into a couple of bugs. I help him troubleshoot for a while, eventually realising that the issue is with the PDF we downloaded (the issue ended up related to the network blocking certain wget operations - something I didn't even know was possible).

After class, I visit a friend of mine. He brings me around campus and shows me the Green Library (a large library), inside which there is a section called the West Stacks. The area is temperature controlled and I would guess contains lots of old knowledge. He also brought me to get some food which I thought was not too bad at all! We chilled at another building for a while before I took the bus home.

I would say my main takeaways from my time here are the following two things.

One: I think that it's really sweet that a lot of these adult learners are still continuing to hone their craft even when they're retired or don't need it for work. Another person I met had the intent to create a chatbot to help her child. There is something almost hopeful in seeing that people really never stop learning.

Two: This wasn't from the lesson but rather from meeting some Stanford students after the lesson. I realised that it compounds. The time you put into things. It just compounds and compounds and compounds and it never really stops compounding. When I was around them they would be talking about the latest paper on XYZ - the type of conversations you'd expect people who were extremely interested in their craft would have. I admire their passion. My environment is really different, and that's not really in a good way. But I landed myself here!

I observe that people often justify the time spent in their youth that did not directly contribute to their current aspirations as "time that was spent making memories" or "having a childhood". The truth is I don't agree at all. I really regret wasting approximately 4 years of my life doing nothing - most of it spent essentially being very unhappy. There weren't a lot of memories made grinding away on video games or hiding in hallways to sleep. The only tangible result I've gained from that marathon is an overly acute sense of self. Which truthfully the knowledge of that which motivates me and that which doesn't has really only led to some frustrating outcomes. I don't say this to victimise the self - I am the culprit really. I just wish I worked harder. And even though you can't change anything by wishing I really wished I worked harder.

Anyway I don't mean to end off on a bad note. I haven't yet reached the juncture of no return - so I've just gotta get myself together now!
