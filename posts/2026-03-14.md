### Berkeley

Today was a Berkeley tour. The idea for this had emerged during a conversation when I recalled my previous unsuccessful tour attempt, where my mother and I had driven an hour to reach campus, and once parked, both succumbed to the fatigue that accompanies lenghty drives. Upon waking, we decided it was too dark to see anything and drove another hour straight back to the hotel.

My general consensus after taking the BART was that it really wasn't all too bad. Although the train wasn't equipped with any tables like you'd see in the Caltrain, the area was generally clean and seating was plenty. The journey from Berryessa to Downtown Berkeley was around an hour twenty, during which I mostly texted Dyllan about his exciting summer internship plans. 

Once meeting Eugene, our first stop was a coffee house named Binge, where I sampled a $7 strawberry matcha. I found it to be acceptable but maybe not quite bingeworthy (hah). The second activity was a short campus tour, where Eugene, being a previously eminent campus ambassador, described each building's purpose and bits of trivia associated with them.

The next activity was a picnic at the Glade (or as Eugene explained - the heart of Berkeley). While I sat and observed shirtless men tossing a football around and groups of friends seated in circles chatting, I thought about just how surreal this all was. Not because the guys were shirtless, but rather the sheer lightheartedness of that before me was something so seldom seen that it's existence was difficult to process. The sight had faint whiffs of central park last summer.

Eugene and I once discussed which things affected us most. I cited people as the primary instigator. A good day with good company could elevate my mood greatly while the converse also holds true. Eugene, however, cited the weather as one of the most important factors. When we had that conversation I don't think I really grasped what she was alluding to. The weather to me was just something that simply existed. But sitting there then observing the people basking in the sun, I think I finally got it. Anyway I ended up getting a bit sunburnt. My cheeks were red like tomatoes.

Afterwards, Eugene brought me to the Campanile (this ginourmous tower overlooking the entirety of berk). You could kinda mentally map the campus from there. Then, we headed to the Doe library which I thought wasn't too well ventilated. It was kinda like a sauna honestly.

Our last campus venture was into a classroom. It was a very old looking classroom with a blackboard and everything. The next two hours were a result of the semi-random decision to teach one another about our functions within our jobs, illustrated with the help of a blackboard. In my opinion that was a lottt of fun. There's a certain type of charm in teaching your friends that is not quite present in typical lecture settings by a professor.

The night ended with pizza. Which was GOOD. I've kinda forgot what were the exact ingredients on the pizza but the name of the pizzas were planet-themed (the restaurant's name was Jupiter) - the one we ordered was called Xanthia.

I enjoyed today's experience tremendously. Like tremendously tremendously. I suppose the many mostly uneventful weekends had accumulated a heap of restlessness that had been entirely dispelled by today's activities.

 I'm not sure if it was because I had over-romanticised the campus life, but after heading home I did feel faint wistfulness. A small part of me felt regretful that I didn't get to lie down at the glade everyday and sip boba for four years. Maybe hedonic adaption would have kicked in and then I'd just feel the glade was really hot. I don't really know. In the famous self-help book titled *Atomic Habits*, James Clear argues that the environment is the most important factor in behavioural change, beating out even individual motivation in the long run. Although I say I don't really know, I have a faint suspicion that some combination of the physical and interpersonal environment would have contributed to me growth in some positive way. Maybe I'll do my masters here!
